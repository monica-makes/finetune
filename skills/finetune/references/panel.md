# How the inspector behaves

For people, not for runs: how the panel looks and works, measured against Figma UI3. A tuner run reads `tuner-api.md`; a bake reads `bake.md`.

## The inspector

- **Hide UI and its first-run tip.** The first time finetune shows in a browser, a toast in the middle of the window says "Press D to toggle overlays off and on" (the key in the tooltips' shortcut colour) for 4 seconds (once, kept as `finetune:tip:hotkey`; not with `open: false`). Hide UI: hides the panel, the toolbar, the Design / Motion bar, the grid and the selection's outline at once (the selection itself is kept, as Figma's Minimize UI keeps it, and shows again with the rest), leaving only the Hide UI button (at the right of the panel's top row) in a small box where it sat, to bring them back; from fully hidden (`open: false`) it shows everything. Figma's ⌘\ and ⇧⌘\ (Ctrl on Windows) do the same, and the toolbar's tooltip shows the key.
- **Toolbar** (left), as Figma's left rail, slimmed (her call, 2026-10-14): icons only, each tool a 32 box with its 24 icon at 1:1, 4 apart and 8 in from the rail's edges, their names in their tooltips. First finetune's mark (the "ft" ligature at 20px, her outline mark drawn as a 1px line like the kit icons, the fill mark on hover; its tooltip reads "View the repo" and a click opens the repo in a new tab), then a short grey line (16 × 1, `--ft-i-line`), then Copy, Save and Reset; hover greys the box and nothing stays picked. Copy changes (its tooltip reads "Copy changes and prompt"), Save (its tooltip reads "Save changes in this browser") and Reset (its tooltip reads "Reset to the code's last presets"), greyed out with nothing to reset (moved from the panel's top row 2026-10-14). **Hide UI** (the hotkey, T by default, or ⌘\ / ⇧⌘\) sits at the right of the panel's top row; hidden, it stays in place as a small box of its own. The top row stays put when the panel scrolls. Popovers and menus have 13px corners (UI3's radius-large); the panel, toolbar and bar have 8px. A panel button stays highlighted (UI3's selected pair: bg-selected behind its icon in icon/selected; UI3's pink pair in Motion) while its menu or popover is open or its toggle is on; an eye stays highlighted while its layer, fill or effect is hidden. The panel's scrollbar floats over its content, as Figma's does (the content keeps its 240 when the panel scrolls): a 4px pill with no track, 2px in from the panel's right edge and 4px from its top and bottom, that drags like a scrollbar, and the panel doesn't bounce at its ends (or scroll the page once it reaches one). They're actions, so none stays pressed. Hovering one shows its name. Undo and Redo sit at the left of the panel's top row. **Design** and **Motion** (Figma's Design and Prototype) sit in a bar at the bottom of the window, as Figma's toolbar does, each with its icon and name; the chosen one is filled with the accent, its icon and name white, as Figma's selected tool (blue, pink in Motion). The bar takes finetune's theme, as the rest does (the next bullet); Design shows everything below; Motion is the next bullet. The bar hides with the panel.
- **Theme, colours and icons: Figma UI3's** (the UI3 kit's Colors, effect styles and Icons, 2026-10-14). Every colour is a UI3 colour token, light or dark, named in PANEL LOOK's comments; shadows are UI3's elevation styles (200 the bar, 300 tooltips, 400 popovers, menus and the controls panel); menus, tooltips and toasts stay dark in both themes, as UI3's are. The theme follows the system (`prefers-color-scheme`) until **⌥⌘T** (Ctrl+Alt+T elsewhere) flips it; the flip is kept in this browser (`finetune:theme`), and a flip back to the system's theme goes back to following the system. It's set as `data-ft-theme` on `html`. Two are finetune's own (her calls): the panel's container is pen.dev's (`#F8F8F8` with a `#E3E3E3` border in light; UI3's `#2C2C2C` and `#444444` in dark), and Motion's accent is UI3's pink where Design has its blue. The overlays on the page (selection, hover, marquee, size label, component outline) take UI3's canvas colours, which follow the theme. Icons are the UI3 kit's, 24 frames shown at 1:1 (never scaled; a smaller slot centres one with negative margins), the panel's small variants where Figma's panel uses them. Three aren't in the kit and stay Phosphor's, at 16 inside a 24 frame: Save's floppy disk (her pick) and Undo / Redo's U-turn arrows. Reset is UI3's restart arrow (her pick). Flow's four icons are composed from UI3 pieces, because the kit has no symbol for Figma's 2025 Flow row: the rounded squares of `autolayout-wrap`, and a 1px arrow with a compact chevron head. Constrain proportions is UI3's `link-broken` when off and `link-connected` when on. Clip content's tick is UI3's 16px `check`. Sizes follow UI3's grid (its Spacing page): controls 24 tall in 40px heading rows, list rows 32, corners on UI3's radius tokens (2, 5, 13); the 8px floating boxes, the bar's 6px inset and what was measured off Figma's own panel (the 48px header, the picker's 4px corners, its 12px gaps) stay as they are.
- **Aligning to columns** (Design, while a Horizontal layout shows). A click on empty space inside one of its columns (the page itself under the pointer, not a gutter or margin) picks that column: it's outlined as a selection is, and hovering the page outlines the column a click would pick. Most pages leave no empty space in a column (sections, cards and images run edge to edge), so **⌥-click** (Alt-click elsewhere) picks the column under the pointer whatever layer covers it, and holding ⌥ outlines that column instead of the layer; **⇧⌥-click** adds a column or takes it back off. Shift-click adds more columns, or layers (or takes one back off). While columns are picked, a shift-click on the page takes the outermost layer under the pointer that doesn't span the whole guide (its first column's left edge to its last one's right), so the heading or the header block around it is picked rather than the full-width section it sits in (inside a layer she has drilled into, the search starts below it, so the click stays at her level); ⌘⇧-click still takes the deepest layer. The kind line names them: "Column 3", "Columns 3–5" (in a row) or "Columns 2, 5", "of 12", and the layer count on the right. A click on that line opens the Page settings, where the Horizontal layout lives (the columns and layers are let go). Just columns: Figma's help card ("Aligning layers to a column", the column grid icon, "Shift-click layers to align them to this column. Shift-option-click more columns to align across them or match their width." (shift-alt-click elsewhere); several: "Aligning layers to columns", the columns icon, "Shift-click layers to align them to these columns or match their width."), no close. Columns and layers: **Position** with **Align to column(s)**: left, center and right line the layers up with one column's edges and middle, or with several columns' leftmost edge, middle and rightmost edge (⌥A ⌥H ⌥D too; a group moves as one), its how-to under it (words only, no icon, her call 2026-10-14; with several columns it reads "Line layers up with the leftmost, center, or rightmost column.") with Figma's trailing close (24 icon button at the panel's right edge, level with the first line; closed, it stays closed for this session: `finetune:tip:align` in sessionStorage). With two or more columns, **Layout** follows: the layers' **Dimensions** (W and H, Mixed when they differ) and **Make selected N cols wide** (N is the span, the columns between included; was "Match selected to N cols" until 2026-10-14): each layer takes the span's width and lines up with its left edge, sized as a typed W is (see Resizing: what's inside wraps; an image, video, `<svg>`, vector or a box the code gives an `aspect-ratio` keeps its proportions, so its height changes with its width, as does a layer with its lock on). A ⌘G group is one box, as Figma resizes a group: each of its layers' widths and their places across the group scale by the span over the group's width, and the group lines up with the span's left edge, so the layers keep their order and spacing rather than landing on each other, its how-to under it, words only, with the same close (`finetune:tip:fitWidth`). The button ends where the W / H row does, clear of the 32 trailing column; a how-to without a close takes 8 more right padding so its text has 16 on both sides. Escape, a click elsewhere or switching to Motion lets the columns go, as does hiding or changing the layout so they're gone. Each layer an Align or Make wide moved remembers which columns it went to, of which grid (the page's own, named by its classes, while the guide is on its numbers; otherwise her guide's numbers), and Copy notes it under the layer's line for as long as the layer still sits there (`/* div.span-7: columns 3-5 of 12 (.wrap.grid), full span, at 1440px wide */`; `.wrap.grid, not its item` when the layer isn't one of that grid's items; `, fluid columns` when that grid's count comes from its own width, `auto-fit` or `auto-fill`; `, before its column 3 so the grid moves it to the next row` when a `grid-column` starting there would make the grid's auto-placement put it on a new row), so the bake can place it in the grid. Only a layer that sits on the columns by itself is noted: the layers of a ⌘G group moved or sized as one each fill just their part. While a layout shows and nothing is selected, a card sits at the bottom of the panel with the full how-to ("Option-click a column to select it, and shift-option-click to add more. Then shift-click layers to align them to the columns or match their width."; Alt-click and shift-alt-click elsewhere) and Figma's close; closed, it stays closed in this browser (`finetune:tip:column`). Adding a layout (Add layout, or ⌃G with none), changing a layout's numbers (once they've stood still for a moment), or aligning or matching to columns of a layout that isn't a saved style yet (nor the page's own grid) shows a toast for 15 seconds, once a visit per layout: "Use this new layout guide as active default?" with **Page** and **Website** (Use as active grid's "Just this page" and "Apply to all pages": either makes it the active grid; neither is the primary) and a close. The tips say "layers", Figma's word for what's on the canvas.
- **Spacing and type, measured against Figma's own panel (2026-10-14).** The panel's content is 240 wide (the panel 242 with its border), on every page: the panel is `border-box` itself, so a page with no box-sizing reset gets the same sizes as one with Tailwind's preflight: fields 88, 8 apart, the action column 24 at the right, 16 in on the left and 8 on the right. Section titles are 11px/550 in a 40px band; rows start 4 under it; a section ends 16 under its last row. Field labels are 9px on a 14px line, weight 450 (UI3's body/small), in the secondary color; two fields with a label between them sit 24 apart (26 after a row without a label); rows without labels sit 8 apart. The selection's header is 48 tall with its name at 13px/550 on a 22px line, tracked -0.25% (UI3's body/large/strong). Fill and stroke rows end at 172 when they have two buttons (4 apart). The checkbox is 16, with a 6 corner. Constrain proportions (beside W and H) wears UI3's link icon, broken when off and joined when on. Flow's track fills the two field columns (its cells equal), and Wrap sits in the 24 action column beside it; when Wrap is hidden the slot stays, so the track keeps its width. On auto layout, Alignment's box is 64 tall in the left field column (one grey field, its 16px glyphs centred in nine equal cells) with Gap beside it at the top (a caret at the field's end, like the font size's; with Wrap the two gaps stack, 8 apart); Padding's two fields fill the field columns with Individual padding in the action column, and its side fields open below two by two (left and top, then right and bottom), as the corners do. The color picker: a 208 square with 4px corners, then two 16px tracks 12 apart beside the eyedropper, then the hex row, each 12 apart; knobs are 16 with a 4px white ring. Menus: items 24 tall, 4 in on the left and 8 on the right, a line between groups with 7 above and below; the blend modes are in Figma's groups. Corner smoothing's slider is a 16px pill with a 16px knob. Motion follows the same rhythm: Playback speed is a section with its name in the 40px title band and the speeds 4 under it; each group of an animation's settings starts 12 under its line and ends 16 under its last row; its side labels are 11px in the secondary color. Independent corners sit two by two in the field columns, Corner smoothing beside the bottom pair.
- **Motion** (Figma's Prototype tab, true to the code). It's for tuning motion the code already has (one she described to Claude Code, say, whose timing or easing doesn't feel right yet), not making it from scratch. It reads CSS animations (`@keyframes`) and JavaScript ones made with `element.animate()`; what it can't read (libraries that animate frame by frame themselves, like GSAP or most of Motion for React, and CSS transitions) is in the README's "Motion: what it can tune". For one layer, or a group of layers that run the same animation (or none yet: a ⌘G group, made this visit or before, acts as one unit): its kind line first, then **Playback speed**, then its **Animation**. Several layers show only their kind line: motion is set one layer at a time. With nothing selected: **Animations**, then Figma's help card "Adding motion to layers" ("Select a layer on the page, then use Add CSS animation to give it new motion.") with a close that keeps it closed in this browser (`finetune:tip:motion`).
  - **Playback speed for debugging only** (under the kind line, while an animation is selected; its label reads as the kind line's name does; each cell's tooltip reads "Test in 0.25×" and so on): 0.25×, 0.5×, 1× and 1.5×, Alignment's segmented cells spread across the row. A debugging aid for watching motion slower (or faster): the selected animation's layers play at that speed (CSS and JavaScript, one replayed or started later included; a rate the page set itself is multiplied, so 0.5× of a page's 2× runs at 1×) while the rest of the page keeps its own. It's never an edit: nothing is saved or copied, and leaving Motion or the selection puts them back to the rate each had (only the animations it changed).
  - **Groups, read from the code**: layers that run the same animations (the same `animation-name` list, or a JavaScript call's same keyframes, as "the intro rises in" over three lines or a staggered menu) are one group: outlined, picked, listed and tuned as one. Its kind line reads "Group", how many layers and the animation (`3 layers · hl-rise`). A layer with an animation of its own is a group of one, shown as that layer.
  - **Picking in Motion**: hover and a click find the nearest layer with motion under the pointer (a CSS `animation`, or a JavaScript animation finetune has seen) and take its whole group; anything else is picked as in Design (the outermost layer below the level she's in, a ⌘G group as one), so an animation can be added to it. A click on nothing clears the selection. A press only selects: moving, the marquee, resizing, grouping and text editing are Design's. Switching to Motion turns a selection with motion into its whole group, and keeps any other. The selection has no size label in Motion (the size is Design's). Every group on screen is outlined (pink at 50%, one box around all its layers), each box following its layers as they move; the groups are read again every second.
  - **Motion's accent** is UI3's pink (pink-500: `#FF24BD`, `#F316B0` in dark) where Design uses its blue: the selection, hover and size label, field focus, the bar's chosen cell, the picked row, a menu's highlighted item and the curve (`html.ft-motion` is set while Motion shows). Every popover and menu closes when the tab changes.
  - **Adding an animation**: a layer (or group) with none shows one full-width button, **Add CSS animation** (Figma's secondary button), which opens the list to pick from (only a layer without one gets it; one that has animations tunes those): the page's own `@keyframes` first, under "Your tokens", then, under a line, Figma's generic ones under "Presets" (Dissolve, Move in, Move out, Push, Slide in, Slide out; Instant and Smart animate have no CSS of their own). Each generic one fades or moves up; Direction and Distance turn it afterwards. Picking one adds it to every layer (a group's all at once, so it moves as one unit) with Figma's default timing, ease out over 300ms, filling both ends; each layer's `animation-*` lists get one more entry. A generic one becomes a new `@keyframes` on the page, named for it (`slide-in`, or `slide-in-2` when taken), kept in finetune's own stylesheet (`#finetune-keyframes`) and in the edits, so Save, undo and Reset carry it; Copy lists it whole as `@keyframes slide-in (new)`, her later changes to it included. One that nothing runs any more (another was picked in its place in **Keyframes**) is taken off the page and out of the edits, so Copy lists only the ones a layer runs, and picking it again takes its name back.
  - **Animation** (when the layer has a CSS `animation` or a JavaScript one): Play / Pause and Replay in its header (they drive the page's animation; they aren't edits). Replay plays the animations again from the start (a group's together, so its stagger plays), a one-shot that already finished included (its `animation-name` is switched off and back on, which isn't an edit); Play resumes a paused one or replays a finished one, and the icon follows the animation as it starts and ends. Every edit in its settings replays the layers too, so a one-shot that already ran shows the change (a burst, like a curve being dragged, replays once it settles), then one row per animation, as Figma lists a layer's interactions: its `@keyframes` name and duration (a JavaScript one: the properties it animates, or its `id`, and "JS · 400ms"). A row opens its settings in the panel, under it (one at a time; a click on the row closes them; with one animation they start open), laid out as Figma's Interaction popover, label on the left, in groups split by a line. The layers on the page are the preview: every edit replays them.
    - **Keyframes**: which `@keyframes` play, from the same list as adding one (the page's own, then the generic ones; a JavaScript animation's are in its call, so this is a label). Then only what applies, read from the keyframes (a fade has no direction; a roll left has one): **Direction** (Left, Right, Down, Up; which way it moves, from its first keyframe to its last, or out to its farthest and back) and **Distance** (how far, in its unit) when it moves; **Scale** when it scales; **Rotation** when it turns; **Opacity** when it fades. Each reads the keyframe farthest from rest (scale 1, no turn, opacity 1); an edit changes that keyframe (every keyframe at that value). A field typed back to rest (Opacity 100%, Distance 0) stays, so it can be typed again: it edits the keyframes it did before, and a Distance typed out from 0 moves them as they moved, in the Direction shown (the one they had; a pick sets where the next Distance goes). A direction turns every keyframe's move (flipped along its axis, swapped across it); a distance scales them all. Moves, scales and turns are read from the `translate`, `scale` and `rotate` properties or the `transform`'s functions, and written back the same way; one set with a `var()`, a `calc()` or mixed units isn't offered.
    - For a group, every edit goes to all its layers, and a value they don't share reads Mixed. **Delay** is when the group starts (its first layer's delay; typing shifts them all) and **Stagger** the step from one layer to the next in the order they start (by delay, then by their order in the code; typing spaces them evenly; Mixed when the code's steps aren't even).
    - **Easing** (the curve in front of its name; Figma's presets: Linear, Ease, Ease in, Ease out, Ease in and out, Ease in back `cubic-bezier(0.3, -0.05, 0.7, -0.5)`, Ease out back `cubic-bezier(0.45, 1.45, 0.8, 1)`, Ease in and out back `cubic-bezier(0.7, -0.4, 0.4, 1.4)`; and **Custom bezier**, which shows the curve editor: two handles to drag, x from 0 to 1, y from -0.5 to 1.5), **Duration** and **Delay** in ms.
    - **Iterations** (a number, or ∞), **Playback** (CSS's `animation-direction`: Normal, Reverse, Alternate, Alternate reverse) and **Fill mode** (None, Forwards, Backwards, Both).
    - **JavaScript animations** (`element.animate()`): finetune wraps `animate()`, so it sees every call made after it loads (one running from before is found through `getAnimations()`). An animation is known by its keyframes as the call gives them (or its `id`). Edits go on its running copies and on every later call, so they survive the page running it again: timing (a group layer's delay is its own), and keyframe values by index. A layer the page animates again with fresh values (a pointer follower calling `animate()` every frame) is one animation, not one per call: an earlier call on it of the same properties that is over is let go, unless she has edited it. Copy lists it as `animate() <properties>`, the layers it runs on, the function that calls it when the browser names one, and each change as was → now. Replay plays its copies again (a page's `onfinish` handler runs again too).
    - Timing edits are the layer's `animation-*` longhands, one value per animation in `animation-name` order (`animation-duration: 1000ms, 1s`); like every edit, they replay the layers. What the keyframes do is written into the page's own `@keyframes` (live, in its stylesheet), so every layer using them follows, as the code would; Save, Copy and undo carry them like any edit.
  - **Animations** (nothing selected): one row per group on the page, leading with what's animating (its layers by class: the class its group's layers don't all share, so three lines read apart; or id, or tag), then the animation it runs; a click selects the group. None: "No animations running on this page."
  - **Not started yet** (under it, when there are any): CSS animations the page's stylesheets give layers that aren't running them, because the rule needs a class the page adds later (on scroll, on a click). A rule counts when its selector, less one of its classes, matches layers on this page; that class is what starts it, on the layers that hold it in the selector. Each row has **Play**, which adds that class where the page would and selects what it animates, ready to tune. That's the page's own state from then on (a reload puts it back), not an edit. Rules for other pages, inside an `@media` or `@supports` that doesn't hold here (another screen size), or started by a hover or an attribute, aren't listed.
  - A layer with a transition animates Design edits too, as the code would. Dragging, resizing and a typed Spacing don't wait for it (the layer has `data-ft-still` while it moves; Spacing measures each layer after the one before it moves), and the selection follows a layer that moves on its own or is moved by something else (a sibling growing, an image loading, an ancestor animating), the panel's numbers catching up when it lands.
- **Nothing saves on its own.** Edits show live but stay in memory until Save puts them in localStorage (per page, `finetune:edits:<path>`). A reload before Save drops them. In a single-page app, a move to another page that calls `finetune()` there switches to that page: the last page's edits come off whatever of it is still showing, that page's saved edits go on, undo starts fresh, and Save, Reset, Copy's header and "This page" styles use the new path; a `layout` the last page declared goes with it unless the new call declares one. Reset forgets the saved ones and reloads the page as written; then a toast over the bottom bar says "Your edits were reset to what's in the code" with **Undo** for 10 seconds before it fades. Undo brings back what showed before Reset, as it was: what Save had stored goes back in storage, and unsaved edits come back unsaved (one more reload, then "Your edits are back"). Save with no edit at all stores nothing and says "No changes to save" (or, over an earlier save, clears it, with Undo). With nothing to reset (no edit on the page and nothing Save stored), Reset is greyed out, as Undo and Redo are with nothing to step to. (`finetune.reset()` called then says "No edits to reset: the page already matches the code") While Reset's toast shows, Undo in the panel's top row and ⌘Z undo the Reset too. Every action that can be undone says so in a toast with **Undo** (and a close ×) that stays 10 seconds before it fades: Save (Undo puts back what the browser had stored before; the edits on the page stay), Reset, ⌘G and ⇧⌘G, Align all items (no toast when nothing moved), Use as active grid (Undo puts back the active grid before and any layout Save kept for the page, and removes the style if it saved one), Create style and deleting a style (Undo removes or restores it), deleting layers, and ⇧A and ⌥⇧A ("Added auto layout", "Removed auto layout"). An edit's toast (⌘G, ⇧⌘G, Align all items, deleting layers, ⇧A, ⌥⇧A) undoes that edit: it closes as soon as a newer step lands or an undo moves off it. Toasts that can't be undone (Copy changes, notices) stay 1.8 seconds. Toasts sit above the Design / Motion bar.
- **Edits stay with the page's own elements** (React apps and other generated sites). Retyping words (a double-click, or Enter on a text layer) refuses words the code builds from several parts (`Plan {name} pricing`), with a toast "Editing this component would break the code. Change it with Claude Code." (her wording, 2026-10-17): React keeps its own hold on each part, and swapping them blanked the page at its next update. Words React holds as one piece keep React's own text node, and Enter's line break isn't allowed in them (a toast says so). When the page makes an element afresh (a re-render that remounts it, a route coming back), its edits, deletes and ungroups go back on the new one within a frame, and a component's look on its new instances, with no undo step. Each edit remembers which element it was made on (where the code writes it, its React list key, else its first words): after the page changes (a section added above it, a list item moved) it goes back on that element, and when none matches it isn't put on another one: a toast on load says "N saved edits no longer match the page" and Copy notes it. Words the page rewrites after she retyped them (an `{expression}`, a script's counter) get a note under the kind line, "These words are built in code: the page rewrote them.", and one in Copy.
- **Figma's shortcuts**, wherever finetune does the same thing, each shown in its button's tooltip (Mac's symbols on a Mac, Ctrl, Alt and Shift elsewhere): ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z undo and redo; ⌘G and ⇧⌘G group and ungroup; ⌘\\ Hide UI; ⌥8 and ⌥9 Design and Motion; ⌥⌘S Save; ⇧⌘E Copy changes; Delete and Backspace delete the selection; ⌘D (Ctrl+D) duplicates it, taken only with layers selected, so the browser's bookmark key stays otherwise (Figma's Export key: ⇧⌘C is Chrome's Inspect element, ⌘S its Save Page As, so neither is taken over). In Design, with a layer or a selection: Enter selects its children (on text, edits the words), Shift+Enter and \\ the parent, Tab and Shift+Tab the next and previous sibling (see Groups below; ⌘\\ stays Hide UI); ⌥A, ⌥H and ⌥D align left, horizontal centers and right; ⌥W, ⌥V and ⌥S top, vertical centers and bottom; on several layers that aren't one ⌘G group, ⌃⌥H and ⌃⌥V distribute horizontal and vertical spacing (Alt+Shift+H and Alt+Shift+V elsewhere) and ⌃⌥T tidies up (Ctrl+Alt+T); ⇧H and ⇧V flip; ⇧A adds auto layout (Figma's, inferred from how the layers sit: direction, gap, padding, alignment, so nothing moves where the gaps are even; on a layer holding others, or on two or more layers or a ⌘G group, which get a group's) and ⌥⇧A removes it (Shift+A and Alt+Shift+A elsewhere; a page whose Hide UI `key` is `a` keeps ⇧A for auto layout while a layer is selected, and hides on it only with nothing selected, since that key ignores Shift); ⇧⌘H shows or hides; 1 to 9 and 0 set the opacity, 10% to 100%; the arrow keys nudge 1px, 10px with Shift; K turns Scale on and off (V, Figma's Move tool, also leaves it; see Scale below). ⌃G (Ctrl+Shift+4 elsewhere) shows or hides the layout grid, Figma's Show layout grids (on a Mac ⌘G alone groups, so ⌃G is free): turned on with nothing selected, the panel shows the Horizontal layout (brought back if hidden, in Design if Motion was showing), with a layer selected it stays as it is, and with no layout yet it adds the usual 12 columns; the grid's eye shows the key. finetune's own (Figma has none): ⌥⌘T flips the theme. None fires while typing in a field (one inside a web component's open shadow root included) or in words being edited. A key finetune acts on with a selection (Escape included, when it steps out or deselects) is marked handled (`preventDefault`) and stops at `document`, so a page's own shortcuts on `window` don't run it too. A mouse click on a panel or toolbar button leaves no focus on it, so Space or Enter meant for the page can't press it again; keyboard focus shows a 1px blue ring.
- **Undo and redo** sit at the left of the panel's top row (also ⌘Z and ⌘⇧Z, Ctrl on Windows; a field or text being typed in keeps its own). Each change is one step once it settles, so a drag, a color scrub or a burst of typing undoes as one; hover previews never count. Undoing past the first edit puts back exactly what the page had. A value an animation or transition holds isn't written: a toast counts them and Copy lists them (on the layer K is on too, when an animation moves it in px). Refused, with a toast: a turned or skewed layer, text being edited, the page; starting to edit words (Enter, a double-click) leaves Scale. At 0.5x a 1px border is 0.5px in Copy, but Chrome draws it a whole pixel, so the layer can look a pixel off on screen. It leaves with the section's ×, K again, V, Escape (the first Escape leaves Scale with the layer still selected, the next steps out as usual; with the presets menu open, Escape closes the menu first), Motion, or another selection. An undo or redo that lands on something Scale made picks up that factor and its snapshot again. Any other edit while it's on (Layout's W and H) keeps the factor and takes a new snapshot there, with 1x moved so Width and Height read the new size; a move needs none. In the anchor box the arrow keys step from the cell with focus. The section's second row (Scale, Anchor point) sits 3px lower than the other sections' rows, as Figma's does.
- **Vectors** (SVG shapes: path, rect, circle, ellipse, line, polyline, polygon, g, use) are objects with what SVG can do. A click picks the outer `<svg>` (kind SVG, handled like a box); double-click goes in. Moves are `translate` in the SVG's own units (a viewBox can scale them), so a 50px drag moves 50px on screen. Rotate and flip set `transform-box: fill-box` + `transform-origin: center` so the shape turns in place. Size: a rect's `width` / `height`, a circle's `r`, an ellipse's `rx` / `ry`, anything else `scale` with `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` so its stroke keeps its weight; the top-left stays put. Corner radius only on a rect (`rx` / `ry`). **Fill** is the SVG `fill` (+ adds Figma's D9D9D9; a gradient or pattern from the code shows but isn't edited); **Stroke** is `stroke` + `stroke-width`, always Center (SVG draws it centered); **Effects** are only Drop shadow and Layer blur, as `filter: drop-shadow()` (half Figma's blur: drop-shadow's reaches twice as far) and `blur()`. Words inside an SVG are text: their color is `fill`, their alignment `text-anchor`, and they have no box to size. **Icons**: an `<svg>` whose shapes paint with `currentColor` (Phosphor's, Lucide's) shows its color as its **Fill** (the svg's CSS `color`), a line icon's **Stroke** as just its **Weight** (`stroke-width` on the svg, in the icon's own units: Lucide's 2; a shape with a `stroke-width` of its own, as Figma's export writes on each path, isn't reached by the svg's, so it gets the weight itself; typing the code's weight takes the edit off), and no Corner radius or box stroke. Any `<svg>`'s **Effects** are Drop shadow and Layer blur as `filter`s, so a shadow follows the drawing, not its box. An icon's weight or which icon it is are props in the code (Phosphor's `weight`), not CSS, so the panel doesn't change them.
- **Dragging moves, as in Figma.** Press on a layer and drag: it's selected and follows the pointer, and so does the rest of the selection when she presses inside one (a group moves as one; a layer inside another selected layer rides along with it). Each layer's move is a `translate`, so it's an ordinary edit: the Position fields follow it live, the whole drag is one undo step, and Copy and the bake carry it. A press inside a bigger selection that ends without a drag narrows the selection to what she clicked. Shift-click never drags.
- **Groups, as in Figma.** Shift-click adds a layer to the selection or takes it out. A drag that starts on the page draws a marquee and selects every layer it touches that a click on the page would pick, live (a group as one); shift-drag adds to the selection, and a click without a drag clears it. **⌘G** (Ctrl+G) groups the selection (not a layer with a layer it holds: the bake's one new element can't wrap both); **⌘⇧G** ungroups it and leaves its layers selected ("Ungrouped N layers" with Undo), or ungroups a container from the code (see Ungroup a container below). Their notices: "Select two or more layers to group.", "One of these layers holds another, so they can't be grouped.", and "Select a group to ungroup." (nothing selected, several layers that aren't a group, or a layer that holds nothing). A click on any layer in a group selects the whole group (the hover outlines it as one); double-click goes into the group and selects that layer, double-click again edits its text, and Escape steps back out a level. Two or more selected show "Group" or "N selected" with **Alignment** (Figma's six: Align left, horizontal centers, right, top, vertical centers, bottom; each lines the layers up on an edge or the middle of the selection's box, a selected group moves as one, and a selection that is just one group lines up its own layers; each click is one undo step, and the moves are ordinary `translate` edits) and X and Y, which move them all together, and Align items to nearest column moves a group as one. Several layers that aren't one ⌘G group ("N selected") also get UI3's **More options** (the `more` dots at the end of the Alignment row, its 24 action column), a menu of **Distribute horizontal spacing** (⌃⌥H), **Distribute vertical spacing** (⌃⌥V) and, under a line, **Tidy up** (⌃⌥T), each with its keys at the right as Figma's menus show them. Each unit counts once: a ⌘G group whose layers are all selected moves as one, a layer inside another selected one rides along. Distribute keeps the leftmost and rightmost units (topmost and bottommost) where they are and spaces the ones between evenly; it takes three ("Select 3 or more layers to distribute"). Tidy up reads the units into rows and columns (a unit joins one when either middle falls inside the other); one row is spaced along at its most common gap from its leftmost unit, each keeping its height on the page (one column the same way down); otherwise they become a grid from the selection's top left, a unit's column its place in its row, each column as wide as its widest unit and each row as tall as its tallest, at the most common gap across and down (a direction whose layers all overlap takes the other's). The most common gap is counted to the nearest px and kept at the code's own value (7.7 stays 7.7, so nothing gains a sub-pixel move); overlaps don't count. When the units sit in one row or column, Position also shows **Spacing** (UI3's `al.spacing-horizontal` or `al.spacing-vertical`, by the axis), the gap between them, or Mixed; typing a number moves each one after the first so every gap is that number. It shows and hides as the layers move (a Distribute or Tidy up that lines them up shows it at once). All of these are ordinary `translate` edits, one undo step each, saved and copied like any move; Copy lists each moved layer's `translate`, nothing else. A layer that a ⌘G group's auto layout places, selected without the rest of its group, would only go back where the group puts it, so they refuse with "Auto layout places these layers" and Spacing hides. The live page is never rewrapped (that breaks a React page): a group is how layers select and move here, saved with Save, and Copy lists it as `group N  a, b, c` (in the code's order, not the order she picked them) for the bake to wrap in a real layer. Saves keep each group's auto layout (`autoLayout`); older saves still load. Ungrouping takes a group's auto layout with it, its layers keeping their moves; a regroup that changes a group's layers drops its auto layout.
- **The cursor** is Figma's canvas arrow while the inspector shows (UI3's Move tool icon, black with a white rim), so the page reads as being in finetune's mode; words being edited keep the text cursor. On a corner handle it is the resize cursor: the macOS diagonal arrow with a line outside each head that matches the head's size and angle (NW-SE on nw/se, NE-SW on ne/sw), with the browser's own resize arrows as the fallback. On a side handle (left and right, top and bottom) it's the same two heads turned level or upright, without the line outside each head: only Scale's cursor has that (her call, 2026-10-17). On a side it is the same glyph turned level (left and right) or upright (top and bottom), in a 32 box with its hotspot at 16 16 so the heads fit. In a rotation zone it is her rotate cursor (a quarter arc with a head at each end, hotspot 12 12), its bend pointing away from the selection at each corner, and it stays on the whole page while a turn is dragged.
- **Delete** (Delete or Backspace, Figma's keys) deletes the selected layers, a ⌘G group's included, and toasts "Deleted N layers" with Undo (⌘Z too). The live page is never taken apart (that breaks a React page): a deleted layer is hidden (`data-ft-deleted`), saved with Save, and Copy lists it as `<selector>  delete` for the bake to take out of the code. A deleted layer leaves any ⌘G group it was in. Inside a group's auto layout the remaining layers close up, as they will in the code; a group left with one layer loses its auto layout with it. Reset brings it back.
- **Duplicate** (⌘D, Ctrl+D elsewhere, Figma's Duplicate selection) copies the selected layers: each gets a live clone right after it (after that original's other copies, so copies keep their order), and the copies are selected in place of the originals, ready to move; "Duplicated N layers" with Undo. Of a selection where one layer holds another, only the outermost is copied (its copy already holds what's inside); a ⌘G selection's copies come as a ⌘G group of their own (its auto layout isn't copied). The copy is finetune's own node, never the code's (as a delete never takes the page apart): a clone of the original as edited, marked `data-ft-dup`, its ids stripped (no two elements share one, and the page's own scripts keep finding only the original), tracked under a minted key (`<original's key> /* dup n */`) so it survives Save, reload and a React re-render — remade from the original as it stands, its own edits put back on. Edits on the copy and on layers inside it are the copy's own, never hunted for elsewhere (`inDup`). Copy lists it as `<selector>  duplicate of <selector>`, its own edits on the same line and its layers' on theirs, for the bake to copy the element in the code; its `@` is the original's place. Deleting a copy takes it out whole (no `delete` line; the edits inside it go with it), and a copy can be duplicated again (its line names the earlier copy as its original). A copy has no React fiber, so it doesn't follow a later component edit to its original's component: it keeps the look it was cloned with. When that leaves it lagging (the original's component-painted values differ from the copy's, and the copy's own edits don't say so), Copy adds a note under its line saying the baked copy — a real instance — takes the component's look by itself, so the bake writes nothing extra for it.
- **Ungroup a container** (⇧⌘G on one layer the panel reads as Group: an HTML element in the code that holds others, not the page). As Figma's Ungroup: its box goes and its layers stay exactly where they are on screen, then they're selected. The live page is never taken apart (that breaks a React page): the wrapper gets `display: contents` (`data-ft-ungrouped`), so it draws no box, and any layer that moved or resized when the box went is put back with ordinary recorded edits, measured before and after. That's its own layers and everything around it: in a grid or a flex row its layers take cells or slots of their own and push the rest along, and in plain flow its padding goes, so the other layers in its parent are measured and put back too, and the parent keeps its size so nothing after it on the page moves. The edits are a `translate`, and a fixed `width` / `height` where a size changed (with `flex-shrink: 0` in a flex row, as Figma's Fixed; a layer the wrapper stretched that would collapse to nothing takes its measured size). Its layers count through a container already ungrouped inside it (that one draws nothing; its own layers are measured, put back and selected), and the panel's "N items" counts the same way. It's one undo step, toasted "Ungrouped N layers" with Undo; Save keeps it, Reset brings the wrapper back, and Copy lists it as `<selector>  ungroup` (its layers' and its neighbours' edits on their own lines) for the bake to take the wrapper out of the code. Live, its layers still inherit from it (`display: contents` keeps it in the DOM), so Copy adds `<selector>  carry to children: white-space: nowrap; …` when it hands them something the bake would lose: her own edits to an inherited property on it (a color, a font), and any inherited value it sets that its parent doesn't and a layer of it still reads. The wrapper leaves any ⌘G group it was in. A link (an `<a>` with an `href`, an `<area>`, or `role="link"`) is never ungrouped, since its box is what its layers are clicked through: the toast says "This group is a link and cannot be ungrouped here." with a close × (6 seconds), and nothing changes. The wrapper's own look (a fill, border, padding, shadow) goes with its box live; the bake asks before losing it in the code.
- **Inline text** (a span) ignores move, rotate, flip and size, so the first of those edits also sets `display: inline-block` on it. That's in the changes list too.
- **Fields only commit a typed change.** Clicking into a field and out again records nothing.
- **T dims the panels** (her ask, 2026-10-17): the panel, the toolbar, Hide UI and a tuner's panel go to 20% and let the pointer through, so what they cover can be seen and picked, moved and edited (the page doesn't zoom out from under them as Figma's canvas does). T again brings them back. Only while the panel shows (hidden or minimized, they come back undimmed), not while typing, and not when a Hide UI key of `t` already has T.
- **E hides the panels, editing goes on** (her ask, 2026-10-19: they sat over what she was working on): the panel, the toolbar, the Design / Motion bar, Hide UI, a tuner's panel and any open popover go (hidden, not taken out, so the panel keeps its layout; `html.ft-bare`), while selecting, dragging, resizing, retyping, every shortcut, the selection's outline and the grid stay. E again, the hotkey (D) or ⌘\\ brings them back. From hidden or minimized, E shows the inspector with its panels already hidden. Entering it drops T's dim and the focus a panel field had. Not while typing, and not when a Hide UI key of `e` already has E.
- **The covered-layer tip** (her ask, 2026-10-19): a press on the panel, the toolbar, Hide UI or a tuner's panel, on their empty space or words (not a control), over a layer they cover shows a toast for 5 seconds, "Press E to hide the panels and keep editing, or T to see through them" (the keys in the tooltips' shortcut colour). Once a visit.
- **A press on the selected layer keeps it** (her ask, 2026-10-17), as in Figma, however it was picked (a click, a ⌘-click, the layer menu, a group's list): her level becomes its own, so the press doesn't go back out to the layer around it, and a double-click on selected text starts editing at once, the whole text even where the pointer is on a word set apart inside it (a `<strong>`, a link; an icon, image, control or block inside it is still gone into). **A double-click on words edits them at once, however deep they sit** (her report, 2026-10-17: a name two containers in took a click and three double-clicks), as long as they're not in a ⌘G group she hasn't entered (that double-click enters the group first); on anything else a double-click still goes one level in; a double-click on its edge, where the resize handles sit 4 in over it, edits it too. Hovering it outlines nothing new, as the press keeps it.
- **Number fields take maths, as Figma's do** (her ask, 2026-10-17). Every number field (the fill row's opacity included) works out a typed sum before it reads it: `+`, `-`, `*`, `/` and brackets (`60-4` is 56, `1112/2` is 556, `(100-8)/2` is 46), keeping the unit (`12%+4` is 16%, `45°+15` is 60°, `0.3s*2` is 0.6s). Any unit works (`10vh+5` is 15vh). What isn't a sum reads as typed: a plain number (`-4` is minus 4) or a word (Auto, Mixed, a hex). A sum that can't be worked out changes nothing and the field shows its value again, as Figma's does: one cut short (`16-`), units that differ (`1s+200ms`), or a unit only after the first number (`24+10%` would turn a 24px line height into 24%). The colour picker's channels and a tuner's number boxes take sums too (a tuner's box is a text box for it, with the phone's full keyboard; ↑ and ↓ still step it by its `step`; a refused or unfinished sum, and Escape, put back the value from before the typing), and a guide field shows a sum live once it adds up.
